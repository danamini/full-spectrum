# Spectral data credits

Generated by `tools/build_spectral_data.py` (run with `python3 -I tools/build_spectral_data.py --raw "$(mktemp -d)"`).
Licence terms checked 2026-10-20.

- **`data/cie1931.js`: CIE 1931 2° colour-matching functions** (x̄, ȳ, z̄), 360–830 nm at 1 nm, rounded
  to 4 significant figures. Source: CIE dataset "Colour-matching functions of CIE 1931 standard
  colorimetric observer", https://files.cie.co.at/Publications-datasets/CIE_xyz_1931_2deg.csv
  (landing page https://cie.co.at/datatable/cie-1931-colour-matching-functions-2-degree-observer).
  Citation: CIE (2019), doi:10.25039/CIE.DS.xvudnb9b; from CIE 018:2019 Table 6, ISO/CIE 11664-1:2019.
  The script checks the published SHA-256. **Licence: CC BY-SA 4.0** (stated in the CIE metadata file).
  You need attribution, and **ShareAlike applies**: this derived file must also be released under CC BY-SA 4.0.
  The same table is on CVRL as `ciexyz31_1.csv`. The values are identical, but CVRL gives no explicit licence.

- **`data/cones.js` (`CONES`): Stockman & Sharpe (2000) 2° cone fundamentals**, linear energy units,
  normalised to peak 1, 390–830 nm at 1 nm. Source: CVRL, Colour & Vision Research Laboratory, UCL,
  http://cvrl.ucl.ac.uk/database/data/cones/linss2_10e_1.csv. Citation: Stockman A, Sharpe LT (2000).
  The spectral sensitivities of the middle- and long-wavelength-sensitive cones derived from measurements
  in observers of known genotype. Vision Res 40:1711–1737, doi:10.1016/S0042-6989(00)00021-3. The same
  functions are the basis of CIE 170-1:2006. The CVRL file leaves the S-cone column blank above 615 nm,
  and the script stores those values as 0. **Licence:** CVRL publishes no explicit licence. It is a free
  research database, and the expected practice is to cite the original paper and CVRL. Redistributing a
  small derived table with that citation is normal practice, but no written permission exists.

- **`data/cones.js` (`ANIMALS`): other animals' photoreceptors.** Values come from the papers:
  - Honeybee 344/436/556 nm: Peitsch D et al. (1992) J Comp Physiol A 170:23–40, doi:10.1007/BF00190398.
    The abstract gives only the across-species clusters (340/430/535 nm). The *Apis mellifera* values are
    the widely cited figures from the paper's tables. I did not check them against the full text.
  - Dog 429/555 nm: Neitz J, Geist T, Jacobs GH (1989) Vis Neurosci 3:119–125, doi:10.1017/S0952523800004430
    (abstract-verified).
  - Goldfish UV cone, about 355–360 nm (stored as 358): Bowmaker JK, Thorpe A, Douglas RH (1991) Vision Res
    31:349–352, doi:10.1016/0042-6989(91)90087-L (abstract-verified). The other goldfish cones are not included.
  - Mantis shrimp, 12 receptor types spanning 300–720 nm, with poor colour discrimination: Thoen HH et al. (2014)
    Science 343:411–413, doi:10.1126/science.1245824 (abstract-verified).
  - Pit organ (pit vipers, pythons, boas): Gracheva EO et al. (2010) Nature 464:1006–1011,
    doi:10.1038/nature08943 (abstract-verified). Detection works by radiant heating through TRPA1 channels,
    not by a photopigment. The paper gives no wavelength range, so `rangeUm` is null.
  These are facts and short citations, so no licence issue arises.

- **`data/lines.js`: visible emission lines and Fraunhofer lines.** Air wavelengths, 380–750 nm, for H I, He I,
  Na I, Ne I and Hg I (the strongest lines by NIST relative intensity: up to 25 for Ne, 10–15 for the others).
  Intensities are normalised to the strongest kept line of each element. NIST intensities depend on the light
  source, so compare them within one element, not across elements. Fraunhofer wavelengths come from NIST ASD
  (Fe I, Mg I, Ca I and Ca II as well). Observed wavelengths are preferred, and Ritz wavelengths are used only
  as a fallback. Source: NIST Atomic Spectra Database, lines form
  https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/ASD/lines_form.html (CSV output of
  https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/ASD/lines1.pl). Citation: Kramida A, Ralchenko Yu, Reader J, and NIST ASD
  Team (2024). NIST Atomic Spectra Database (ver. 5.12), https://physics.nist.gov/asd, doi:10.18434/T4W30F.
  **Licence:** ASD is NIST Standard Reference Database 78. Under the Standard Reference Data Act
  (15 U.S.C. §290e), NIST holds copyright in SRD. It is not public domain, unlike most NIST data. NIST asks
  that SRD products carry the statement: "Copyright protection on this compilation of data has been secured
  by the Secretary of the U.S. Department of Commerce on behalf of the United States in the United States
  and all countries that are parties to the Universal Copyright Convention, pursuant to Section 290(e) of
  Title 15 of the United States Code. NIST Standard Reference Data (SRD); © 2024 by the U.S. Secretary of
  Commerce on behalf of the United States of America. All rights reserved."
  (https://www.nist.gov/open/copyright-fair-use-and-licensing-statements-srd-data-software-and-technical-series-publications).
  A small cited extract like this one is ordinary scholarly use. Show the citation and the statement above in the app's credits.
  **Fraunhofer designations** follow the conventional table reproduced in
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraunhofer_lines (CC BY-SA; we use only the letter assignments, which are
  facts), which goes back to Fraunhofer (1817). Some letters are ambiguous: "d" can mean Fe 466.81 nm or
  He 587.56 nm, and "e" can mean Fe 438.36 nm or Hg 546.07 nm. Both readings are included and flagged. A, B and a are telluric O₂ bands from
  Earth's atmosphere. They are molecular and therefore not in ASD, so their wavelengths (759.370, 686.719,
  627.661 nm) are the tabulated values, marked `src: 'table'`.

- **`data/solar.js`: ASTM G173-03 reference solar spectra.** Extraterrestrial (AM0, Gueymard 2002 synthesis)
  and global tilt (AM1.5G, 37° sun-facing surface), 280–4000 nm, W m⁻² nm⁻¹, 4 significant figures.
  Values are bin-averaged: 1 nm bins at 280–1000 nm and 10 nm bins at 1010–4000 nm, so the integrals are
  preserved. Source: https://www.nlr.gov/grid/solar-resource/spectra-am1.5 (file
  https://www.nlr.gov/media/docs/libraries/grid/zip/astmg173.zip, containing `ASTMG173.csv`). NREL was renamed
  the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR), and www.nrel.gov no longer resolves. Citation: ASTM G173-03(2020),
  Standard Tables for Reference Solar Spectral Irradiances: Direct Normal and Hemispherical on 37° Tilted
  Surface, ASTM International; spectra computed with SMARTS2 v2.9.2 (Gueymard C, 2001, Solar Energy
  71:325–346). **Licence:** NLR provides the tables freely, with the condition "By accessing these files, you
  agree to abide by the NLR data disclaimer" (https://www.nlr.gov/disclaimer). The disclaimer covers DOE
  contract terms, no warranty and no endorsement, and does not restrict redistribution. The ASTM standard
  document itself is copyrighted by ASTM. We redistribute only the numerical spectrum, which is widely
  republished, for example in pvlib. Credit ASTM G173-03 and NLR/NREL.
